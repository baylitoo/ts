\begin{abstract}
Real-world banking AI must satisfy \emph{deployability} constraints---replayability, privacy, fairness, auditability, and latency---not just accuracy. We present \textsc{HARBOR}, a minimal set of \emph{system contracts} and \emph{governance telemetry} for temporal heterogeneous-graph (THG) workloads (fraud/AML, routing, screening). \textsc{HARBOR} standardizes (i) replayable temporal slices with provenance fingerprints, (ii) encoder adapters for drop-in temporal GNNs, (iii) risk-aware policy heads using QR-DQN/CVaR with governance-ready logs, and (iv) guardrail metrics spanning privacy, fairness, audit completeness, replay fidelity, and latency. We instantiate the contracts on two AML corpora and show how class-prior preservation and unlabeled-node handling integrate into deployable evaluation. This is a position paper: we specify interfaces, risk objectives, and measurement protocols so future models can plug into banking guardrails with minimal glue code.
\end{abstract}

\section{Position: Deployability as a First-Class Contract}
\label{sec:position}
Temporal heterogeneous graphs (THGs) arise across banking workflows: transactions, counterparties, suppliers, and sanctions with typed nodes/edges and continuous-time events. Most THG models (e.g., TGN, DyRep, EvolveGCN; \citealt{rossi2020tgn,trivedi2019dyrep,pareja2020evolvegcn}) optimize representation quality but externalize deployability (privacy, fairness, latency, audit). Our position: \emph{treat deployability as a stable systems contract that outlives any particular encoder or RL algorithm}. 

An anonymized reference implementation (bundled with the submission) instantiates these contracts in a modular stack: a configuration-driven pipeline factory, dataset/feature registries, encoder and agent adapters (GraphSAGE, GAT, an internal multi-branch encoder, QR-DQN), and governance hooks that plug into an internal governance gateway (details anonymized). \textsc{HARBOR} specifies the shared substrate that both standalone experiments and governed deployments consume.

We organize the position around three axes mirrored in the codebase: (1) \textbf{dataset fabric} that guarantees replayable temporal slices with provenance and class-prior controls, (2) \textbf{model adapters} that keep encoders/agents swappable while exposing risk measures, and (3) \textbf{governance plane} metrics and telemetry that make policies certifiable for production.

We propose \textbf{\textsc{HARBOR}}---a small set of mandatory I/O fields and telemetry that any dataset slice, encoder, and policy must emit. This enables:
(i) immutable audits and exact replays,
(ii) risk-aware control bound by policy directives,
(iii) portable privacy/fairness checks,
(iv) apples-to-apples latency measurement.

\section{System Contracts (Stable Interfaces)}
\label{sec:contracts}
Implementations may vary; \emph{contracts do not}. Given raw temporal events, the Dataset Fabric emits:
\[
(G, X, Y, M, \Pi, \phi),
\]
where $G=(V,E,t)$ is the time-stamped graph; $X$ are features; $Y$ labels (possibly missing); $M$ are masks for splits and label-availability; $\Pi$ is the active \emph{directive set} (policy IDs, e.g., block-rate caps, fairness thresholds); and $\phi$ is a provenance fingerprint:
\[
\phi = \mathrm{SHA256}\big(\texttt{schema.json} \Vert \texttt{raw\_hashes} \Vert \texttt{config} \Vert \texttt{seed}\big).
\]

\paragraph{Encoder Adapter.}
Maps $(G,X,t)\mapsto h_t \in \mathbb{R}^d$ and implements
\texttt{state\_dim()} plus \texttt{explain($h_t$)} $\rightarrow$ temporal attributions (feature importance with timestamps).
Adapters cover GraphSAGE, TGAT/TGN, or temporal transformers.

\paragraph{Agent Head.}
Maps $(h_t,\Pi)\mapsto a_t$ and emits \emph{telemetry} 
$\tau=\{\text{logits or quantiles},\ \text{directive\_id},\ \text{thresholds},\ \text{version},\ \phi\}$ \emph{before} actuation. Telemetry is immutable and stored with retention consistent with banking policy.

\paragraph{Reference Implementation (Anonymized).}
The pipeline assembles these contracts from configuration dataclasses: an \texttt{ExperimentConfig} specifies dataset/feature/agent choices, loaders materialize AMLNet/Elliptic graphs, feature extractors add temporal + network descriptors, and encoder/agent registries instantiate the matching adapters. Each run persists the manifest (\texttt{config.json}), the provenance fingerprint $\phi$, and the directive set $\Pi$ alongside checkpoints. CI checks verify contract compliance (field coverage, provenance hashes, redaction policies).

\section{Risk-Aware Control: QR-DQN with CVaR}
\label{sec:risk}
We expose risk controls through distributional RL. Let $\mathcal{Z}_\theta(s,a)$ approximate returns via $N$ quantiles $\{\hat{Z}_{\theta,i}\}_{i=1}^N$ \citep{dabney2018distributional}. With targets $\{z'_j\}$ from $r+\gamma \mathcal{Z}_{\bar\theta}(s',a^\star)$, the quantile Huber loss is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{QR}}(\theta)=\frac{1}{N}\!\sum_{i=1}^N \sum_{j=1}^N
\rho_{\kappa}\!\left(z'_j - \hat{Z}_{\theta,i}\right)\!
\Big|\tau_i - \mathbb{I}\{z'_j - \hat{Z}_{\theta,i} < 0\}\Big|,
\end{equation}
with $\tau_i=\frac{i-0.5}{N}$. For risk-sensitive action selection, we use the CVaR tail:
\begin{equation}
a^\star = \arg\max_{a}\ \text{CVaR}_\alpha\!\left(\mathcal{Z}_\theta(s,a)\right)
= \arg\max_{a}\ \frac{1}{\lfloor \alpha N \rfloor}\!\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor \alpha N \rfloor}\hat{Z}_{\theta,(i)}(s,a),
\end{equation}
where $\alpha\in(0,1]$ is boundable by governance directives (e.g., conservative tails for certain jurisdictions). See \citet{chow2015risk} for CVaR in RL.

\section{Guardrail Metrics (Algorithmic, Systemic, Societal)}
\label{sec:metrics}
We require all runs to report:

\textbf{(M1) Guardrail Coverage.}
\% of directive IDs exercised at least once in the replay suite.

\textbf{(M2) Audit Completeness.}
Share of required fields present in $\tau$ and $\phi$; target $100\%$ (fail-closed if missing).

\textbf{(M3) Replay Fidelity.}
$\mathrm{EMD}\big(\pi_{\text{live}}(\cdot|s),\pi_{\text{replay}}(\cdot|s)\big)$ averaged over sampled states (frozen seed/config).

\textbf{(M4) Latency Bins.}
End-to-end online inference (encoder + policy + governance write): p50/p95/p99 with batch size, window size, model size, GPU.

\textbf{(M5) Privacy Telemetry.}
PII redaction recall/precision, language breakdown, and reviewer-effort deltas (hours saved) under documented policies.

\textbf{(M6) Fairness Drift.}
Per-jurisdiction (or sensitive proxy) disparity of FPR/TPR at a fixed operating point; report equalized-odds gap and trend.

\section{Reference Implementation \& Production Readiness}
\label{sec:implementation}
\textbf{Modular pipeline.}
\texttt{ExperimentConfig} + the pipeline assemble dataset loaders (AMLNet, Elliptic, synthetic), feature extractors (temporal statistics, network motifs), encoder adapters (GraphSAGE, GAT, internal multi-branch), and agent heads (DQN, QR-DQN) into a reproducible training stack. Swapping any module is a configuration change; manifests are persisted automatically.

\textbf{Governance hooks.}
Each training/evaluation run emits immutable manifests, provenance hashes, and telemetry payload schemas. Checkpoint managers embed the directive set $\Pi$, the fingerprint $\phi$, and QR-DQN risk settings inside artifact metadata so external governance gateways can validate candidates before deployment.

\textbf{Testing + CI (in-flight).}
Unit/integration suites enforce contract compliance (field coverage, replay fidelity, latency collectors). Automated provenance checks, schema linting, and differential testing replay stored manifests to guarantee determinism.

\textbf{Operational roadmap.}
Asynchronous audit spooling with bounded queues, manifest signing, deploy-ready container images, and scripted canary rehearsals are included in the roadmap (details anonymized for review).

\section{Case Slice: AML under Label Scarcity}
\label{sec:aml}
\paragraph{Class-Prior Restoration.}
Labeled-only AML slices inflate fraud priors versus production reality. We enable \texttt{--include-unlabeled}: unlabeled nodes are masked in supervised loss but remain navigable for RL exploration. Let $\hat{p}_{\text{fraud}}=\frac{1}{|V|}\sum_v \mathbb{I}[y_v=1]$. We report $\hat{p}_{\text{fraud}}$ (Wilson 95\% CI) before/after and enforce batch priors within $\pm\epsilon$ via a sampler that respects temporal coherence and entity-disjoint splits.

\paragraph{Risk-Aligned Rewards.}
Hybrid rewards combine high-confidence TP bonuses, calibrated FP penalties, and exploration bonuses for rarely visited intermediaries; coefficients are bound to business directives in $\Pi$ and recorded in $\tau$.

\paragraph{Encoder/Agent Choices.}
The repository packages RMGANets with an adapted multi-branch loss and QR-DQN heads tuned for heavy-tailed returns. Ablations cover SAGE/GAT baselines, RMGANets with/without auxiliary branches, and risk measures (mean, median, CVaR$_\alpha$). All runs share the same contracts, enabling guardrail metrics to compare architecture swaps fairly.

\paragraph{Feature Integrity.}
Versioned tests assert stable IDs/dtypes/timestamps and persist normalization stats so encoders observe consistent distributions despite upstream drift.

\section{Societal Impact \& Governance}
\label{sec:societal}
\textbf{Privacy.} We log masking policies and language coverage, and require auditability of redaction outcomes (M5).\\
\textbf{Fairness.} We expose directive-level constraints and report disparity trends (M6).\\
\textbf{Accountability.} Immutable $\tau$+\,$\phi$ enable post-hoc review; canary replays are mandatory before rollout.\\
\textbf{Human Oversight.} Reject options and escalation routes are first-class actions; measured as part of coverage (M1).

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}
\textsc{HARBOR} assumes reliable timestamps and stable directive registries; unlabeled-node inclusion can bias exploration if unlabeled $\neq$ negative. Governance logging adds modest latency overhead; we mitigate via async writes and bounded queues. This paper specifies \emph{contracts and metrics}; exhaustive encoder/policy benchmarking is out-of-scope for a 4-page position.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:rw}
Temporal GNNs: TGN, DyRep, EvolveGCN~\citep{rossi2020tgn,trivedi2019dyrep,pareja2020evolvegcn}. Distributional RL and risk: \citet{dabney2018distributional,chow2015risk}. Systems for scalable RL: RLlib~\citep{liang2018rllib}. AML on graphs: Elliptic dataset~\citep{weber2019aml}. Our focus differs: \emph{deployment contracts and governance telemetry} for THG workloads in finance.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We argue for deployability-first interfaces for temporal-graph banking AI. \textsc{HARBOR} specifies minimal contracts and guardrail metrics so future encoders and policies remain swappable under privacy/fairness, replayability, and latency constraints. The reference implementation powers internal studies and connects to (but is not subsumed by) an internal governance gateway; ongoing work hardens production checklists (asynchronous audits, manifest signing) and releases adapters plus governance schemas to encourage reproducible deployments.
